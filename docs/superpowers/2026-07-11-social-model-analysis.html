Held observations
  • Both two-sided mechanisms are late additions and group-scoped; nothing in the Direct world asks permission.
  • The picker can target only the inviter's own followers — no invite-by-code exists.
  • The invite-to-group row is owner-only in the UI while rules permit any member to push-invite (js/groupContext.js:138 vs database.rules.json:142).

  • Membership is always written by the joiner themself; nobody can place you in a group.

Where the gates live Every tier restriction is client-UI only. Rules gate on identity, never on relationship, for knock and call (database.rules.json:88-99, 159-169). Functions gate notifications on opt-in prefs, never tier. The social model is enforced by affordance, not by contract.

Both operator-named anomalies confirmed: Direct knock requires mutual (js/following.js:692); roster knock requires only co-membership (js/groupContext.js:219). Call is mutual-only, Direct-only; groups deliberately have no call (js/groupContext.js:198-199).

Presence asymmetry: presence is world-readable at the rules layer (database.rules.json:14) but the UI subscribes only to followees — A follows B ⇒ A sees B, B sees nothing of A.

Long-press exists exactly twice, both palette adoption — a self-affecting act, nothing sent to the other party.

I.4Notifications

One channel per person (bot DM if linked, else web push; never both). Six server triggers only; no trigger watches followers, grants, membership, deletion, or revocations — those are structurally incapable of notifying.

Two socially-loaded facts Warm signals are opt-in; asks are unconditional. And Telegram recipients can act socially from inside the notification (join, approve, knock back) — the bot is a social surface, not just a delivery channel.

I.5Lifecycle — how edges end

  1. Unfollow vs evict wear the same × but differ structurally: self-unfollow leaves the reverse edge; eviction force-drops it via the revocations mailbox — the one place the app reaches into someone else's relationships. Both silent.
  2. Rotation is asymmetric: the new code propagates to people you follow; your followers keep a stale (cosmetic) code forever.
  3. Invite teardown never touches redeemed edges.
  4. Declines don't exist for the other side — the requester's "Requested" state is immortal after a decline (#181 I5).
  5. Kick is half-built: no UI (#180), but the receiving side would toast "You've been removed from '{name}'."
  6. Account-level: graduation moves an identity (edges survive); unlink/replace expunges (every edge silently destroyed on both sides). Web has no account deletion at all.

I.6Surface parity

Interactions have total parity — no Telegram branch touches knock, call, long-press, presence, naming, inbox, or request-to-follow. Formation and management diverge:

The bot is a third surface: it can consent (Join, Approve) and react (Knock back) but cannot initiate edges. A bot-side join auto-names you and auto-broadcasts 2h availability, disclosed after the fact.

IIThe derived value set — for ruling

Reconstructed from behavior, not intention. For each principle: keep / amend (how) / reject.

P1Presence flows toward those who chose you
"You are visible to the people you decided to let watch."

conformsDirect presence subscribed only for followees; followers see nothing back; availability pushes need opt-in.

strainsGroups: joining exposes you to the whole room with no per-person choice — and broadcasts 2h of availability by default.

questionIs group visibility a deliberate second contract ("a room is mutual exposure") or an unexamined leak of the Direct contract?

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P2Possession of the key is consent
"Anyone holding your code may follow you; sharing it is the grant."

conformsCode entry writes instantly; links auto-redeem; follow-back is instant.

strainsRequest-to-follow introduced the opposite model — an explicit declinable ask — but only inside groups. Two consent philosophies now coexist; the circled-plus collision is the visible symptom.

questionIs key-possession canonical (request-to-follow = the exception for people without your key), or is per-event consent the direction of drift?

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P3Naming flows outward; self-presentation is claimed at the doorway
"You choose what strangers first call you; those who know you decide privately what to call you; nobody can rename you."

conformsAll doorway strings self-authored; all ongoing labels viewer-private; notifications speak the viewer's vocabulary.

strainsBare-code sharing has no self-presentation channel; group invites carry no inviter identity; the bot auto-claims your Telegram name; rules would let owners rename members (dormant).

questionIs the doorway-claim + private-label split a value worth defending, or an accident of accretion?

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P4Departures are silent
"Nothing announces an ending."

conformsEverything — unfollow, evict, declines, revoke, rotation, leave. The copy even promises it.

costIllegible states: the immortal "Requested" button; the never-informed inviter; the evicted contact who just stops existing.

questionKeep silence even at the cost of ambiguity — or split "silent to the other party" from "legible to yourself"?

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P5Only warmth interrupts
"A push means presence or an invitation — never bookkeeping."

conformsSix triggers only: knock, call, availability, invite, follow-request. Graph changes never notify.

strainsWithin the warm set: knock/call/availability are opt-in per person; invite and follow-request are unconditional.

questionConfirm the boundary; decide whether "asks" belong in the opt-in regime.

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P6The audience became visible
"You can see who follows you (code and name) — but never what they call you, and following you earns them no visibility of you."

todayFollowers section, follow-back, picker enumeration. The origin's audience-invisibility is gone in behavior; a narrower privacy survives.

questionRatify the current compromise — or is the picker's enumeration of non-mutual followers as invite targets already past the comfortable line?

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P7A group is a room, not a network
"Joining a room exposes you to the room, grants room-scoped gestures, and leaves no trace when you leave."

conformsPer-group self-naming; group-effective availability; roster knock as a room gesture; request-to-follow as the doorway from room-acquaintance to direct bond; clean evaporation on leave.

strainsUnder P7 co-member knock is coherent, but call's absence from rooms needs a reason, and the owner-only follower-limited picker is a network-shaped restriction on a room-shaped feature (rules already allow any member to invite).

questionIs the room metaphor canonical? If yes: member knock ✓, owner-only invite ✗?, call-in-rooms = deliberate absence or gap?

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P8Reciprocity earns intimacy
"The closer gesture requires the more mutual bond: watch < knock < call."

conformsIn Direct the ladder is real: following grants seeing; mutuality grants knocking and calling.

collidesCo-member knock jumps the ladder. P7 and P8 are both live and they disagree about knock — the operator's original anomaly, restated.

questionWhen P7 and P8 conflict, which wins? Or are they scoped (P8 governs Direct, P7 governs rooms) — and the scoping is the grand rule?

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P9The invitation carries the warmth
"Relationships arrive pre-named because someone reached out; cold adds arrive bare."

conformsLinks carry creatorLabel; grants carry name+code; Telegram publishes first names both directions; bare-code adds attach nothing.

strainsGroup links carry no inviter identity; the in-app "invite to follow me" surface is missing (link and share-sheet only).

questionComplete the principle (inviter identity on group invites; an in-app invite surface), or is anonymity-by-default fine?

RULING: keep / amend / reject
P10The model is enforced by affordance, not by contract
"The UI is the ruleset; the database trusts the client."

todayEvery tier gate is client-side; rules check identity only. For a small trusted user base this is a choice with social meaning.

questionRatify (eyes open — #288 F2 shows where it bites), or treat capability rules as contract-worthy once they carry social meaning? Shapes whether Phase 2's target model speaks only of UI or also of rules.

RULING: keep / amend / reject

The three cross-cutting tensions (Phase 2's agenda)

  1. Two consent models (P2 vs request/accept) — visible as the circled-plus collision.
  2. Ladder vs room (P8 vs P7) — visible as mutuals-call / co-members-knock.
  3. Silent endings vs legible state (P4) — visible as the immortal "Requested" and the never-informed inviter.
